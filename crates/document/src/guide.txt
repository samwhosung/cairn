cairn zone — build a zone of its own from the command line

A zone is a directory. `cairn zone new` or `replay` makes one; every other verb changes it or asks
about it. Each change is a line of the zone's journal, with its author and time, and each author
undoes and redoes their own steps. Each verb that changes the zone writes the files cairn draws
to ZONE/build as it ends, so `cairn shot --zone ZONE/build`, `cairn view --zone ZONE/build` and
`cairn atlas --zone ZONE/build` show it. The verbs do what you say, where you say it, and nothing
else; a scatter picks its spots, models, facings and scales from its own words and the rules the
catalog gives its models, the same each time, and the journal keeps those rules.

Who: --as NAME on any verb, else $CAIRN_AUTHOR, else your login name. A name is a letter, then
letters, digits, _ or -.

The catalog: --catalog DIR on any verb, else $CAIRN_CATALOG: the catalog `cairn catalog` wrote,
whose table of each model's rules a scatter keeps.

Where: yards in the zone's own frame, x east and y south of its north-west corner; z is a world
height. A tile is 533.33 yd square, a chunk 33.33 yd. A facing is a compass bearing of a model's
front (0 north, 90 east).

Make
  cairn zone new ZONE --tiles 2x2 --height 40 --texture PATH --borrow Elwynn Forest
                 [--effect N] [--name NAME] [--origin X,Y]
      --borrow: the install's zone whose light, sky, water colour and music it takes
  cairn zone set ZONE [--start X,Y[,FACING]] [--borrow ZONE] [--name NAME]

Ground. A brush is --at X,Y --radius R, or --line X,Y X,Y ... --width W; --falloff 0 is a hard
edge, 1 eases from the middle (0.5 if not given). An AREA is a brush, --rect X,Y X,Y or --poly
X,Y X,Y X,Y ..., whose falloff only a brush has.
  cairn zone raise ZONE 6 BRUSH           cairn zone lower ZONE 6 BRUSH
  cairn zone flatten ZONE 42 BRUSH [--strength 0..1]    or, without a height, along a --line
                                          to the heights its points carry (X,Y,Z), else the
                                          ground's under them
  cairn zone smooth ZONE BRUSH [--passes 4] [--strength 0..1]
  cairn zone roughen ZONE 2 --size 12 BRUSH [--seed N]
  cairn zone relief ZONE Redridge Mountains AREA [--strength 0..4] [--falloff F] [--seed N]
      adds the relief of the install's zone of that name, such as Elwynn Forest or Alterac
      Mountains: its ground less its own smoothed shape, laid in patches 67 yd across blended
      into each other. The shape already there stays under it

Paint. A chunk holds at most four textures: a stroke that would bring a fifth leaves that chunk
alone and says which; --make-room drops the chunk's least-used other texture instead.
  cairn zone paint ZONE PATH AREA [--strength 0..1] [--make-room] [--slope 35..90] [MASK]
  cairn zone texture ZONE PATH --effect N     its ground effect: the grass it grows and the
                                              sound of steps on it

A range is LO..HI, LO.. or ..HI, both ends included. --slope is a paint's range of the ground's
slope in degrees, from the corners of its cell, and a scatter's rule for its models. A MASK keeps a
stroke or a scatter to part of its area:
  --height ..48         the ground's height in yards
  --water 3..           yards from the water's edge, less than 0 in the water
  --on PATH ...         only where these textures cover the ground: fully where they cover
                        three quarters of it or more, not where a quarter or less
  --off PATH ...        only where they don't, eased the same way
  --soft 4              each range's ends, --slope's too, ease over 4 of its own units (degrees
                        or yards), half strength at the end itself

Things. Each gets an id, its author's name and a count (sam.12); on the command line a bare
number is one of your own. A thing stands on the ground (--dz above it, following the ground) or
at a height (--z); --set N is a building's doodad set; --stands leaning tilts a model as the
ground under it is, --stands upright stands it straight.
  cairn zone place ZONE PATH X,Y [--facing D] [--scale S] [--dz D | --z H] [--set N] [--stands S]
  cairn zone move ZONE ID [X,Y] [--facing D] [--turn D] [--scale S] [--dz D | --z H] [--set N]
                  [--stands S]
  cairn zone remove ZONE ID ...
  cairn zone scatter ZONE --models A B ... AREA --count N [--facing D] [--seed N] [MASK]
                     [--apart D] [--scale 0.8..1.2] [--slope 0..30] [--stands upright|leaning]
      Each model keeps its own rules, from where the install places it: the slopes it stands
      on, how far apart, its scales, and whether it leans with the ground. A rule given here
      overrides them: one value for every model, or one for each. A model the catalog doesn't
      know keeps any slope, scale 1 and upright, and needs --apart. The journal keeps every rule
      the scatter kept. A scatter replaces what an earlier one with its seed (0 if not given)
      placed of its models inside its area, so the same scatter twice lands once, and two
      unseeded scatters of one model replace each other where they overlap; a thing placed or
      moved by hand stays. It spreads over whatever else stands there
  cairn zone water ZONE 41.5 AREA         cairn zone water ZONE --remove ID ...

Steps
  cairn zone undo ZONE [N]     take back your last N steps (1 if not given); refused where
                               someone else has changed what the step changed since
  cairn zone redo ZONE [N]     put back your last N steps taken back
  cairn zone run ZONE [FILE]   the verbs above, and undo, redo, here, things and build, one a
                               line without ZONE, from FILE or standard input; each line is its
                               own step, or joins your step before it when it starts with `+`
                               (a drag's many moves, taken back by one undo), and is answered
                               with any lines it prints, then `ok ...` or `error: ...`. From a
                               file, the first error stops the run. --journal-at-end keeps the
                               journal in memory until the run ends, for testing that a crash
                               then loses what the run answered for
  cairn zone batch ZONE [FILE] the lines as one step: all of them apply, or none
  cairn zone replay JOURNAL ZONE   a new zone from a journal's lines
  cairn zone build ZONE        write the zone's files and ZONE/build again

Ask
  cairn zone here ZONE X,Y [--radius R]    the ground's height, slope, paint and water; what
                                           stands near
  cairn zone things ZONE [--at X,Y [--radius R]] [--model WORDS]

The zone's files are yours to read: zone.txt, things.txt (id x y z facing scale set stands
scattered model), water.txt, palette.txt, and journal.txt, every change in order. heights.bin,
paint.bin and history.bin are the ground, the paint and what undo and redo need.
